Sephora Beauty Product Pricing Model

A Gradient Boosting model trained on ~7,800 real Sephora products to predict a "fair" price based on measurable attributes — category, size, formula complexity, brand, and popularity signals. The goal: understand how much of beauty product pricing is explained by measurable product substance versus intangible brand equity.

Key finding Even after controlling for brand identity, the model systematically underestimates prices for top-tier prestige brands (La Mer, SK-II, Guerlain, and others) — and the gap grows with price: products under $50 track close to predicted price, while products over $200 are priced on average 177% above what formula, size, and category alone would predict. This suggests brand equity and positioning — not product substance — become the dominant price driver in the luxury tier.
